Short Form Consent Process for Subjects with Limited English Proficiency

Use this page to download translated short form consent documents for enrolling incidental, unanticipated research participants who have limited English proficiency.

Go to the short form library ↓

Your study must be approved to use the short form consent process.

Approval covers every language for which the IRB maintains a pre-translated short form in its library, except any language the IRB excluded for your study; those need a full translated consent form instead. Your approval letter identifies the excluded languages, so check it before you download. Use of the short form on a study that was not approved for it is noncompliant and must be reported promptly to the IRB.

How approval works

The IRB may approve the short form written consent process for obtaining consent from incidental, unanticipated potential subjects with limited English proficiency. When enrollment of subjects with limited English proficiency is anticipated, a full translated consent form is used instead. Approval to use the short form process is granted at the study level: when the IRB approves your protocol for the short form process, that approval covers all languages for which the IRB maintains a pre-translated short form in the library below. You are not required to specify individual languages in advance.

The IRB may exclude specific languages from short form approval when enrollment of subjects speaking those languages is anticipated at your site; those excluded languages are identified in your approval letter and require a full translated consent form instead. If you encounter a subject whose language is not available in the library, you must obtain a certified translation of the short form and submit it for IRB approval before use.

See the governing policy: Use of Short Form Consent Process (HRPP Policy §8.4.5.2)

Running the consent encounter

The consent process must be verbally translated into a language understandable to the subject, using a qualified interpreter. BMC or Goldman School of Dental Medicine interpreter services, including the contracted telephone interpreter line, may be used, or another interpreter the IRB approved for your study. Minors may not serve as interpreters. Complete the steps in order.

  1. Confirm the participant’s preferred language, that a short form for it exists in the library below, and that the language is not excluded in your approval letter.
  2. Present the short form to the subject in their language.
  3. Using the interpreter, conduct the full consent discussion orally. Begin with the Key Information section at the start of the English consent form, then cover all remaining elements.
  4. Allow the subject to ask questions through the interpreter, and answer them before anyone signs.
  5. If the subject agrees to participate, obtain signatures as described under “Who signs what.”
  6. Provide the subject with a signed copy of the short form in their language and the English consent form.

See the governing policy: Witnesses and Interpreters for Subjects with Limited English Proficiency (HRPP Policy §8.4.5.3)

Who signs what

A short form encounter produces signatures on two documents. The translated short form is signed by the subject (or parent or legally authorized representative) and the witness. The IRB-approved English consent form serves as the English narrative and is signed by the witness and the person who conducted the consent discussion, so it must include the witness signature block.

The witness must be fluent in both English and the subject’s language, and must be present for the entire oral presentation. If the person conducting the consent discussion also provides the verbal translation, a witness who is fluent in both languages and not otherwise associated with the study is required. An interpreter who is not associated with the study may serve as the witness. If a contracted telephone interpreter line is used, the interpreter may direct the person conducting consent to record the interpreter’s identification number on the witness signature line in lieu of a physical signature.

See the governing policy: Witnesses and Interpreters (HRPP Policy §8.4.5.3)

When a full translation becomes required

The short form is intended for incidental, unanticipated enrollment. Repeated use of the short form in a language, or any use in a greater than minimal risk study, means submitting a full translated consent form afterward. The rules below come directly from policy; the risk level of your study is stated in your approval letter.

If your study is… Then…
Any study The short form may be used no more than three times for the same language, unless the IRB approves otherwise. After three uses in a language, you must obtain a full translation of the IRB-approved consent form in that language and submit it for IRB approval before enrolling additional subjects who speak that language. Once approved, offer a copy of the translated consent form to active subjects previously enrolled in that language using the short form.
Greater than minimal risk (in addition to the three-use limit) After any use of the short form, you must obtain a full translation of the IRB-approved consent form into the subject’s language and submit it for IRB approval within 30 days of that subject’s enrollment. Once approved, the translated consent form must be promptly offered to the subject. The IRB may waive or modify this requirement if a full translation would be impracticable.

Submit each translated consent form by amendment, with a Translator Qualifications Form or the translation company’s certificate.

See the governing policy: Use of Short Form Consent Process (HRPP Policy §8.4.5.2)

Reporting and study records

Use of the short form on a study that was not approved for it is noncompliant and must be reported promptly to the IRB. Progress reports and study status check-in reports ask whether any subjects were enrolled using the short form consent process, and if so, how many and in what languages, so the IRB can consider whether the pattern of use indicates that further enrollment of subjects with limited English proficiency should be anticipated. Be ready to say whether you have submitted a full translated consent form for any language that reached the three-use limit, and for any short form use in a greater than minimal risk study.

See the governing policy: Use of Short Form Consent Process (HRPP Policy §8.4.5.2)

Short Form Library

Each short form is a fillable PDF and opens with a cover page of instructions for the research team, which is not distributed to subjects. Before you use a form, complete its fields: principal investigator, IRB protocol number and study title at the top, and the study contact’s name and phone number later in the form. Each field shows an English placeholder in brackets, such as [PI Name] or [Study Title], so you can complete it without reading the language. The study contact field also shows [PI Name]; enter whoever subjects should call with questions. Fill and print the form ahead of the consent encounter. Do not edit the translated text.
